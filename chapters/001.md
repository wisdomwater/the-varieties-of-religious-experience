# Day 1 — The Mystery of Human Experience

*To understand religion, we must begin not with what people are taught to believe, but with what they have actually experienced.*

Human beings have always sought to understand their place within the great mystery of existence. Across cultures and generations, people have spoken of encounters with the divine, moments of profound illumination, and an awareness of realities that seem to extend beyond the ordinary boundaries of life. These experiences have inspired religions, shaped civilizations, and transformed individual lives. Yet beneath the doctrines and institutions that have grown around them lies something more immediate: the personal experience of the human being.

I propose that we begin our inquiry here, not with the authority of religious institutions or the correctness of theological systems, but with the experiences of those who have felt religion most deeply. As a psychologist, I am interested in what takes place within the human mind when a person encounters what they understand to be sacred. What do they feel? How does their understanding of reality change? What influence does the experience exert upon the direction of their life?

We shall discover that religious experience is remarkably varied. Some encounter the divine through joy and gratitude, others through suffering and despair. Some experience a sudden transformation, while others arrive at conviction through years of gradual change. We need not assume that every experience is equally trustworthy, nor must we dismiss what we do not immediately understand.

Our task is first to observe with patience and intellectual honesty. Before deciding what these experiences reveal about the universe, we must understand what they reveal about the people who have lived them. Only then can we begin to consider their deeper significance.
