# Day 21 — When Optimism Refuses to See

*An outlook that preserves happiness by excluding suffering may offer comfort without revealing the whole of reality.*

There is something deeply attractive about the conviction that goodness lies at the heart of existence. We naturally prefer a world in which suffering is temporary, difficulties are opportunities for growth, and every apparent misfortune conceals some greater benefit. Such beliefs can strengthen our courage and preserve our happiness through circumstances that might otherwise overwhelm us. Yet we must ask whether our desire for a comforting interpretation sometimes leads us to overlook what is plainly before us.

The healthy-minded temperament possesses a remarkable capacity to direct attention away from suffering. In its more deliberate forms, this becomes a principle of life: refuse to dwell upon evil, deny it unnecessary influence, and cultivate an unwavering confidence in goodness. Among certain followers of the mind-cure movement, this conviction becomes so absolute that illness and suffering are regarded as little more than errors of thought, possessing no ultimate reality of their own.

I understand the appeal of such an outlook, and I would not dismiss the genuine benefits it has brought to many lives. There are circumstances in which refusing to surrender our attention to misery allows us to recover strength and move forward. But a philosophy must be judged by more than its ability to sustain the happiness of those who embrace it.

Consider the suffering of another human being. We may preserve our own peace by turning away from their distress, but our refusal to acknowledge it does nothing to relieve their burden. Their pain remains a fact of existence, regardless of the interpretation we choose to place upon it.

The difficulty with an entirely optimistic philosophy is that it risks mistaking a selective vision of reality for a complete one. The world contains beauty, generosity, and joy, but it also contains cruelty, loss, and suffering that cannot simply be explained away.

We need not abandon hope in order to recognize these darker truths. But if we wish to understand existence honestly, we must allow the whole of human experience to enter our inquiry, including those realities that disturb our most cherished convictions.
