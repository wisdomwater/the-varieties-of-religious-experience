# Day 82 — The Afterglow of Revelation

*The deepest significance of an extraordinary experience may be discovered not in the moment itself, but in the ordinary life that follows.*

Mystical experiences are often remarkably brief. A moment of illumination may last only a few seconds, while an overwhelming sense of divine presence may endure for minutes before gradually fading. Yet those who undergo such experiences frequently insist that their significance cannot be measured by their duration. Something encountered in a passing moment may continue influencing the individual long after ordinary consciousness has returned.

This enduring influence is among the most important characteristics of mystical consciousness. Unlike a dream that quickly disappears from memory, a mystical experience may leave behind a conviction of extraordinary importance. Although its original intensity cannot always be recovered, the individual continues to understand life through the meaning that the experience appeared to reveal.

The Christian mystic Teresa of Ávila described the remarkable transformation that sometimes followed her periods of spiritual ecstasy. She reported emerging from these experiences with renewed energy, courage, and determination. What had previously seemed difficult or frightening became less intimidating, while concerns that once occupied her attention appeared increasingly insignificant.

Her relationship with worldly honor provides a particularly revealing illustration. Teresa came to regard the pursuit of reputation and social distinction as a distraction from what she believed possessed genuine value. The desire for recognition, which ordinarily exerts such a powerful influence upon human behavior, seemed diminished by an experience that had altered her understanding of what mattered.

She described a similar transformation in her attitude toward wealth. Money and possessions, once capable of attracting considerable attention, appeared less important when viewed from the perspective of her mystical experiences. She imagined how differently people might treat one another if the desire for status and material advantage no longer governed their relationships.

These changes suggest that mystical revelation may influence more than a person's intellectual beliefs. It can reorganize the emotional importance assigned to ordinary circumstances, changing what the individual desires, fears, and considers worthy of pursuit. A conviction that remains merely theoretical may exert little influence upon conduct, while an experience felt with extraordinary immediacy may reshape the direction of an entire life.

Yet we must exercise caution in evaluating these effects. An experience that produces great energy and confidence does not necessarily provide reliable guidance about how that energy should be used. The transformation of character may be admirable, but its value depends partly upon the beliefs and purposes that the experience strengthens.

This distinction is essential because religious enthusiasm can support both generosity and intolerance, both courage and misguided certainty. We cannot determine the truth of a revelation simply by measuring the intensity of its emotional aftermath. Nor should we assume that every enduring change is beneficial merely because it emerged from an extraordinary state of consciousness.

Nevertheless, the lasting effects of mystical experience deserve serious attention. They offer something more substantial for investigation than the immediate sensations of an event that cannot be independently observed. We can examine the individual's subsequent conduct, the stability of the changes, and the ways in which the experience becomes integrated into ordinary life.

The afterglow of revelation therefore returns us to a question that has accompanied our exploration of religious experience from the beginning. Whatever may occur during a moment of mystical illumination, its meaning becomes increasingly visible through the life that follows. The experience itself may pass beyond recovery, but its influence continues to unfold through the choices, relationships, and values of the person who remembers it.
