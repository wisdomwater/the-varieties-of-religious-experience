# Day 4 — Judging a Tree by Its Fruits

*The worth of a belief is revealed not merely in how deeply it is held, but in what it brings forth in human life.*

When we encounter a religious conviction, we are often tempted to judge it by the circumstances of its origin or the intensity with which it is expressed. A person may speak with extraordinary certainty, describe a vision of overwhelming beauty, or claim to have received knowledge from a divine source. Yet none of these qualities, taken alone, can establish the truth or value of what they believe. A conviction may feel profoundly illuminating and still lead us into error.

I propose that we examine religious experiences through three considerations: their immediate power to illuminate the mind, their agreement with reasonable thought, and their helpfulness in the conduct of life. An experience that brings great inward satisfaction deserves our attention, but satisfaction alone is insufficient. We must also ask whether its insights remain credible when examined alongside our other knowledge, and whether the life shaped by them becomes more worthy of admiration.

Consider how readily we accept this principle in other fields. We do not judge a scientific discovery by the temperament of its discoverer, but by the evidence that supports it and the understanding it provides. Nor do we dismiss a work of genius simply because its creator possessed an unusual or troubled mind. We examine what has been produced and allow its merits to speak.

Religious experience deserves neither greater indulgence nor harsher treatment. Its claims must remain open to criticism, and its consequences must be considered with care. A belief that inspires compassion, courage, and generosity has demonstrated something valuable, though these qualities do not by themselves establish every doctrine associated with it.

The deeper significance of religion, then, cannot be settled by tracing its origins or admiring its intensity. We must look at the character of the experience, the reasonableness of its claims, and the fruits it bears in the world.
