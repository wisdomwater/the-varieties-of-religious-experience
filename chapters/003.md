# Day 3 — The Difference Between Explanation and Understanding

*To explain a thing is to describe how it came to be. To understand it is to discover what it means.*

The human intellect possesses a remarkable ability to examine, classify, and explain the world around us. When we encounter something unfamiliar, we seek to place it within a category, identify its causes, and understand the processes that produced it. This is the foundation of scientific inquiry, and it has greatly expanded our knowledge of the natural world. Yet there is a danger in imagining that once we have explained the mechanisms of an experience, we have exhausted its significance.

Consider the experience of religious devotion. A psychologist may describe the emotional processes involved, while a physician may identify the activity of the nervous system. These observations may be perfectly accurate, yet the person undergoing the experience encounters something more than a collection of psychological and physiological events. They may experience forgiveness, discover a renewed sense of purpose, or feel themselves embraced by a reality greater than their ordinary existence.

To reduce such an experience to its mechanisms alone is to mistake a description of its conditions for an understanding of its meaning. We would not dismiss a beautiful work of art because we understood the chemical composition of its pigments, nor would we consider a symphony fully explained by describing the vibrations of its instruments. The explanation is valuable, but it addresses only part of what we wish to know.

I do not suggest that religious experiences should be protected from critical examination. On the contrary, we must be willing to investigate them with the same honesty we bring to every other aspect of human life. But intellectual honesty requires us to recognize the limits of our explanations as well as their strengths.

We may understand the conditions that give rise to an experience without yet knowing what significance it deserves. The deeper question remains open, inviting us to examine not only how an experience occurs, but what truth and value it may hold.
